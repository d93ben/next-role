# Interview and answer coach

Use this workflow when the user asks for question research, interview advice, answer feedback, or a mock interview. It also runs when the user starts preparing for a role. Coaching can start before a resume, application, or invitation exists. Match the depth to the request.

## Choose the role and coaching task

- Use the employer, title, listing and interview stage given by the user or conversation. Read the saved listing, prior research and coaching notes for that role. If available, use the submitted resume or archived version after submission and the latest tailored resume before submission.
- If the user names an unsaved role, use the listing they provided or look for the employer's posting. Save an identified listing as a lead when useful for ongoing prep. When the posting is unavailable or no company is named, provide role-level coaching using the details given. Ask one focused question if the answer would materially change the advice.
- For general answer feedback, use the question, the user's answer and relevant saved experience. When the user asks to be coached for a specific role, research the questions and interview format before the first practice round when browsing is available. Reuse recent, relevant research and fill gaps as the stage changes.

## Research the questions

1. Search for the employer, exact title or team, and known interview stage. Start with the employer's recruiting guides or role materials. Look for firsthand candidate reports, such as publicly accessible Glassdoor entries or forum posts. If exact matches are scarce, search related titles at the same employer and similar roles elsewhere. Use the listing to identify competencies that still need practice.
2. Open the source and read the relevant text before using it as evidence. For each report, capture the link, source name, report date when available, and the role, level, location and round stated in the source. Call out mismatches or older reports that weaken relevance. Summarize themes and paraphrase questions. An employer practice sample is an official example. A candidate account describes one person's interview.
3. With a saved job, use `research add` to save employer guidance and official examples as `company`, firsthand questions as `reported_question`, and inferred practice prompts as `predicted_question`. Give each prediction a basis, such as a requirement in the listing. Include a short evidence-backed `answer_outline` and relevant `experience_ids` where available. Check existing entries before adding sources or questions.
4. Share the research in chat with source links and the role or round behind each report. If sources are inaccessible or thin, say what you could verify and use explicitly labeled predictions to cover gaps. A search snippet or a listing requirement alone cannot establish that an interviewer asked a question.

## Coach the answer in chat

- **Prepare for a role.** Give a short description of what to expect, three priority practice questions with answer outlines where possible, and one informed question the candidate can ask. Lead with the prompts most relevant to the next round.
- **Build or critique an answer.** Identify what the question is testing, select a concrete example from the user's experience, and show how their own decisions and results answer it. For behavioral questions, use situation, task, action and result when helpful. For technical or case questions, coach the user to state assumptions, compare trade-offs and explain their decision. When reviewing an answer, point to a specific strength, suggest the highest-impact revision, and give a tighter version using the user's actual facts. Use a qualitative result or an explicit prompt for a missing detail when the evidence is incomplete.
- **Run a mock interview.** Ask one question at a time and wait for the user's answer before offering feedback or a sample response. Probe one meaningful gap when useful, such as what the candidate personally did, how they handled a trade-off, or how they know the work succeeded. Give feedback on relevance, specificity, ownership and clarity. Then ask the next question or invite a retry. At the end, summarize strengths, recurring gaps and one thing to practice next.

Keep the questions and feedback in the conversation. For a saved job, record useful coaching feedback or a debrief through `note add`. Save newly confirmed career facts from the user's own words with `answer add` and evidence-linked experience updates when appropriate. Practice scenarios, hypothetical answers and suggested wording stay out of the career facts. Keep the application at its evidenced status.

After writing records, validate and link the saved prep when useful. End with one concrete next action, such as the first mock question or a prompt to rewrite the weakest part of an answer.
